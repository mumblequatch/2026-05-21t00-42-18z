Puzzleworld: Player 1

A game by Benjamin and Dad Part 1 of the Puzzleworld series
#game #benjamin #puzzleworld #underworld-adjacent


What the game is

You're a kid in your bedroom. One day, you find a secret way into another world — a place called Puzzleworld. Athena, your stuffed snowy owl, comes alive and flies with you. A bad guy called the Glitch is trying to take over every video game in the world, starting with Puzzleworld. You are the only one who can stop him.
Every level is a different world. Every level gives you one weird object — a shoe, a piece of chalk, something else — and you have to figure out what it can do to solve the puzzles and beat the boss.


The series (three games, one secret idea)

Benjamin figured out the titles, and the titles tell the whole story:

  1. Puzzleworld: Player 1 — Benjamin alone. One player against the Glitch. The Glitch wins (sort of).
  2. Puzzleworld: 2-Player — Mommy and Daddy go in to rescue Benjamin. The Glitch's rule ("there is only one player") breaks. He didn't know there could be more.
  3. Puzzleworld: Multiplayer (or Co-Op) — the whole family, maybe more. The Glitch's rule fully shatters. The world isn't his anymore.

The secret idea: the Glitch wanted to take over every video game and be alone with one helpless player. The series beats him by doing the opposite — every game has more players than the last. The villain loses because he never understood that games can have friends in them.


This is also probably how the final boss works (see Stage 4 — The Logic, below). The Glitch's logic assumes one player. The family breaks the logic by being too many.


Things Benjamin invented

These are all Benjamin's ideas. Dad is just helping build them.

The four secret entrances (and how they work)

Each door in Benjamin's bedroom leads to a different level of Puzzleworld. They unlock one at a time as you play.

  1. The fairy tale book on the bookshelf → Level 1 (the space station).
  2. The closet behind the hanging clothes → Level 2.
  3. The wizard hat → Level 3.
  4. Athena scratching the blanket → Level 4.

⠀The first time you play the game, you don't get to choose. Athena chooses for you — she goes to the book and shows you how to open it. You're new here. She's your guide.
Once you've beaten a level, that door stays available forever. So after you've unlocked all four, you can walk into the bedroom and pick any door you want. The bedroom becomes a hub — every door is a way back to a world you already know.
(That's how Benjamin's original "different entrance every time" idea still works — once everything is unlocked, the choice is yours.)

Athena, the sidekick

Athena is Benjamin's stuffed snowy owl in real life. In the game, she sits on the bed.
The moment you cross into Puzzleworld, Athena becomes a real owl — with real feathers, real wings, and she can talk. She flies next to you for the whole adventure. When you go back home at the end, she becomes a stuffed animal again.

The Glitch

The big bad guy is a talking glitch that breaks the world.
He's scary because he doesn't follow the rules. He can make walls disappear, swap your shoe for a fish in the middle of a level, turn Athena upside down. The only way to beat him is to be cleverer than the rules.
His plan: take over every video game world. He knows the player is the only one who can stop him.

The mini-Glitch

At the end of Level 1, you fight a little Glitch — like the Big Glitch's kid brother. A low-level bug in the code of Puzzleworld.
He's not really scary. He's annoying and broken. He bumps into things. He says the wrong words. He might not even know he's a bad guy — he's just glitching around, getting in your way. Beating him might feel a tiny bit sad, because he's not really a villain. He's just broken.

How the Glitch works (the most important rule)

Fighting the Glitch makes him stronger. Not because he gets mad. Because glitches cause glitches.
When you beat the mini-Glitch at the end of Level 1, the act of beating him is itself a glitch in the code — and that glitch makes the Big Glitch duplicate. Now there are two of him.
When you beat the Big Glitch at the end of PW1, you only beat half of him. The other half is still out there. And that's the half that traps Benjamin in Puzzleworld.
This is the most important rule in the whole game: you can't win by winning. Every time the family fights the Glitch, he gets worse. The puzzle of the whole series is figuring out how to beat something you can't beat by fighting.

How the puzzles work

Every level, you start with one weird object. The whole level is figuring out what it can do.

  • A shoe you can throw. (Throw it at a control panel to break something, so it falls on the boss.)
  • A piece of chalk that draws arrows. (The arrows show you the path through an invisible maze.)
  • More objects Benjamin will invent for each level.

⠀Level 1 — Space station with no gravity
The first level is a space station where there's no gravity. Everything floats. The shoe puzzle happens here. When you throw the shoe, it keeps floating until it hits something, so you have to aim carefully.
The duplication beat: When Benjamin defeats the Big Glitch at the end of PW1, the screen should glitch — music stutters, picture goes wrong for a second — and the player sees, just for a moment, two Big Glitches in the corner of the screen before the level ends. The player knows something is wrong before Benjamin does. (This is what writers call dramatic irony.)


How the Glitch grows (ideas for later games)

These are seeds for PW2, PW3, and beyond. We are NOT building these yet. PW1 only needs Stage 1.
The Glitch evolves every time he's fought. He doesn't just get stronger — he changes form. What he is changes.

Stage 1 — Talking Static (Puzzleworld: Player 1 boss)

The Glitch has a body. He talks. He's scary but knowable. You can see him, hear him, and fight him. You "defeat" him with a puzzle. He duplicates as a side effect.

Stage 2 — Wrong Sounds (Puzzleworld: 2-Player boss?)

The Glitch doesn't have a body anymore. He's a sound. Wrong music, wrong voices, words coming out of the wrong characters' mouths. Mommy speaks in Daddy's voice. Athena's voice plays from a chair across the room. The puzzle of 2-Player might be: which sounds do you trust?
(Dad's note: this is a dream for an audio-drama director to design.)

Stage 3 — Wrong Behaviors (mid-game ofMultiplayer?)

The Glitch isn't a sound anymore. He is the behavior of the world. Enemies don't act like enemies. Doors don't open when you push them. Items do the wrong things. The puzzle: figure out what the world's wrong rule is this time, and use it.

Stage 4 — The Logic (final boss, someday —Puzzleworld: Multiplayer)

The Glitch isn't anything you can see, hear, or touch. He is the game itself. The only way to beat him is to stop playing the game the way the game wants you to be played. Do something the game doesn't have a button for. Break the rules of your own game.
The deep idea: the only thing that can defeat the logic of a game is a player who refuses to follow it.
The Glitch wanted to take over every video game and be alone with one helpless player. The way the family beats him is to be too many. His logic assumes one player. Multiplayer breaks the logic. The Glitch loses because there are too many people in the room.
(Benjamin invented this part. Don't forget.)


Puzzleworld: 2-Player — the rescue (next game, not this one)

At the end of PW1, Benjamin defeats the Big Glitch — but only half of him. The other half traps Benjamin in Puzzleworld.
Puzzleworld: 2-Player is a different game. You play as Mommy and Daddy going in to rescue Benjamin. Two players, working together. The Glitch's rule — there is only one player — breaks the moment they walk in.
Possible big ideas (still being decided):

  • Two-player co-op. Mommy and Daddy each play one character. They have to cooperate to solve puzzles — one of them does something the other can't. (Mommy reads things Daddy can't see, Daddy lifts things Mommy can't, etc.)
  • A broken version of Puzzleworld. The worlds are falling apart because Benjamin (the only real player) is trapped inside. Without a player, the worlds can't hold together. The rescue is urgent because the whole place is collapsing.
  • The Glitch is now Stage 2 — he doesn't have a body anymore, he's wrong sounds. Athena warns Mommy and Daddy: "Don't defeat him. Whatever you do, don't defeat him." And the puzzle is figuring out what that means, because every game ever has trained the player to defeat the boss.
  • A line the Glitch says when he first appears: "There's only supposed to be one player. There's only ever supposed to be one player." And Mommy and Daddy look at each other. Two against one. He doesn't know how to fight two of us.

⠀Still to decide: Athena's role in PW2.

  • (A) Athena is trapped with Benjamin. Mommy and Daddy go in alone.
  • (B) Athena escapes and becomes Mommy and Daddy's sidekick — the bridge between PW1 and PW2.
  • (C) Athena is half-glitched. She appears sometimes to give hints but she's fading. They have to hurry.

⠀(Benjamin picks.)


What we make next

These are the things we're going to build, in order. Benjamin gets to play something new at the end of every step.

Step 1 — Benjamin's bedroom

Build a blocky version of Benjamin's real bedroom in Roblox. Just gray boxes at first — bed, closet, bookshelf, the hook for the wizard hat, Athena on the pillow.
What Benjamin gets to do: walk around the bedroom as his game character.

Step 2 — The first door (Athena and the book)

Make the bookshelf interactive. Athena walks to it, points at the fairy tale book, and Benjamin opens it. The book pulls him in. (The other three doors stay locked for now — we'll unlock them as we build Levels 2, 3, and 4.)
What Benjamin gets to do: open the fairy tale book and watch the world change.

Step 3 — Athena turns real

Build the moment where stuffed Athena becomes real Athena. Wing sound, a little sparkle, and her first line — which Benjamin writes.
What Benjamin gets to do: see his own words come out of Athena's mouth in the game.

Step 4 — The space station

Build the zero-gravity space station. Make the shoe throwable. Make the puzzle work.
What Benjamin gets to do: beat the puzzle he invented.

Step 5 — Meet the mini-Glitch

Build the mini-Glitch as the boss of Level 1. He's annoying and broken — bumps into things, says wrong words, isn't really scary. The "fight" is a puzzle (use the shoe to drop the control panel on him).
What Benjamin gets to do: defeat the mini-Glitch for the first time. (And feel a tiny bit bad about it. That's on purpose.)

Step 6 — The duplication beat

Build the moment at the end of PW1 where the screen glitches and the player sees, just for a second, two Big Glitches in the corner before the level ends. Music stutters. Picture goes wrong. Something is wrong, and the player knows it before Benjamin does.
What Benjamin gets to do: see his cliffhanger work — the moment that sets up PW2.

Step 7 — Share it

Put the game on Roblox as a private link. Send it to three friends.
What Benjamin gets to do: watch someone else play his game.
